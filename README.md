This project was developed as a sample application of concepts learned through a PostgreSQL course. The idea was inspired by a recent family business venture exploring the rental car industry here in Asheville, North Carolina. With an established market dominated by large competitors and seasonal fluctuations in tourism, one potential advantage for a smaller rental operation is maintaining low overhead while making efficient use of its fleet. Asheville's tourism industry is also still recovering from the effects of Hurricane Helene, making inventory management and cost control particularly important. Rather than relying entirely on third-party operational software and recurring service fees, this project explores how an in-house database solution could support day-to-day operations and future business decisions. The database is designed to manage the operational side of a rental car business by centralizing information about vehicles, customers, rental transactions, payments, additional charges, maintenance, and vehicle locations. It allows staff to track who is renting each vehicle, when it is being used, where it is picked up and returned, and its current availability. The database has other uses as well, including tracking maintenance history and associated costs. This information could also help identify differences in demand between locations and guide decisions about where vehicles should be allocated to maximize utilization. One of the keys to success from a business perspective is targeting 80–90% fleet utilization. Seasonal demand creates challenges when determining how much inventory to maintain and where to position vehicles throughout the year. Another important consideration is the lifecycle of each vehicle. Establishing mileage and age thresholds for replacement could help limit unexpected maintenance expenses, reduce vehicle downtime, and maintain a more reliable fleet. Over time, the business could also explore relationships with local dealerships to establish recurring trade-in arrangements and make vehicle replacement more predictable. There are several directions in which this project could develop. One possibility is incorporating an HTML-based interface that allows employees to enter information without interacting directly with the database. The database could also be expanded to include more detailed operating expenses and clearly defined cost variables after consulting with stakeholders. This information could then be translated into an agentic AI solution and visualization platforms like Power BI or Sigma to create a more comprehensive picture of vehicle usage and overall business profitability. This would make it possible to evaluate metrics such as utilization, revenue, maintenance expenses, profit margins, and return on investment. Ultimately, this project serves as a foundation for a more comprehensive fleet management solution, focusing on business intelligence and financial reporting.
